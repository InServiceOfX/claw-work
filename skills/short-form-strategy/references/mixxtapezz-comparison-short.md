## Story

As the person cutting a phone short from a live mix where several MCs ride
one beat, I need the opening to name the beat and ask the question before a
verse runs, and I need the MC’s name to stay readable the whole time that
MC is up.

The reference short does that in the first 7 seconds, as three cards over a
beat that is already playing:

1. Both faces, one word (“this”), arrows naming 2Pac and DMX.
2. The sample, held still: “in the air tonight / phil collins / (1985)”.
3. “better?”

Then one hard cut. DMX gets about 16 seconds, 2Pac gets about 16 seconds.
The artist name and the line they are on stay pinned at the top for that
whole side. The center word changes with the lyric, so a muted viewer still
knows who is rapping. It ends inside the last line, so a replay lands back
on the question. There is no end card and no link, because that channel’s
short is the whole product.

## What to use

- Frame 0 already has a face, the beat, and the claim or the question. The
  words are fully drawn. Eight words is the ceiling.
- The beat or sample is named before the verse body, inside the first 7
  seconds.
- While an MC is up, their name stays on screen, off the eyes, nose, and
  mouth. Clothing or the upper safe area.
- One side change in a 15–30 second short. The roster short is the
  exception: one continuous opening, names counted in order.
- One question. For this mix the question stays “Who did it best?”
- The caption’s first line matches the words already on the frame.

## What stays ours

The audio of our short is one continuous stretch of the live mix. The
reference chops two finished records into lyric cards. That shape fits a
channel with nothing else to watch. Our long mix stays on YouTube, so the
short ends on a card with “WHO DID IT BEST?” and the long URL, sitting
above the bottom caption bar.

## Acceptance

1. A muted viewer can answer “who is this, and on what beat?” from the first
   7 seconds alone.
2. The MC name does not cover the face, and it does not disappear while
   that MC is the one playing.
3. The beat is not cut into a montage of entrances.
4. When a long mix is the thing being shared, the last card carries the
   question and the long URL.
