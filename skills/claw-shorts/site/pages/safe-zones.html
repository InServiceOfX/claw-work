Safe Zones for Captions on YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Reels

Each short-form video platform overlays UI elements on your video—usernames, like buttons, sound info, subscribe prompts. If your captions or important text land under these overlays, viewers can't read them.

This guide covers the danger zones for each platform and provides a tool to check your frames before rendering.

Platform-Specific Danger Zones

YouTube Shorts

YouTube Shorts shows the video title and a subscribe/like row at the bottom. On a 1080×1920 frame:

  • Top danger zone: ~100px for status bar
  • Bottom danger zone: ~288px (15%) for title and buttons
  • Right side: Safe except for the bottom corner

TikTok

TikTok has the most aggressive overlays. The right edge is covered by action buttons (like, comment, share, profile). The bottom holds the username, caption, and sound info.

  • Top danger zone: ~120px for status bar
  • Bottom danger zone: ~480px (25%) for username/caption/sound
  • Right danger zone: ~150px for action buttons

Instagram Reels

Instagram Reels is similar to TikTok but with slightly smaller overlays:

  • Top danger zone: ~150px for status bar and camera button
  • Bottom danger zone: ~384px (20%) for username/audio
  • Right danger zone: ~130px for action buttons

Combined Safe Zone

If you want one video to work well on all three platforms, use the intersection of safe areas:

Edge Margin (px) Notes
Top 150 Reels has the tallest top bar
Bottom 480 TikTok has the tallest bottom overlay
Left 48 All platforms are similar
Right 150 TikTok has the widest right buttons

This leaves a 882×1290 pixel safe area in the center of a 1080×1920 frame. Place all important text and graphics within this region.

Applying Safe Zones in claw_shorts.py

The claw_shorts.py renderer uses these constants for text placement: 

SAFE_TOP = 150      # pixels from top
SAFE_BOTTOM = 300   # pixels from bottom
SAFE_SIDE = 50      # pixels from each side

These values are more conservative than the minimum danger zones, giving extra breathing room. For TikTok specifically, you may want to avoid the right 150px even in the middle of the frame.

Sources

These dimensions are approximations based on:

  • YouTube Shorts ad specifications (2023)
  • TikTok vertical video advertising best practices
  • Meta Business Help Center: Reels ad specifications
  • Direct measurement on iOS and Android devices (2024)

UI layouts change with app updates. Always test on actual devices before a major release.
